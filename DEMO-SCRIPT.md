# Demo video — 2 min 30 s

**Record the laptop, not the phone.** The demo rig sliders are what make both rounds
visible, and they live beside the phone frame on desktop. Film the phone only for the
one Ghost Ping shot at the end, if you have time.

**How to record on Windows:** press `Win + G`, hit the record button, and speak into
your laptop mic. Or `Win + Alt + R` to start and stop directly.

**Before you hit record:**
- Open the Vercel URL, not localhost
- Reload once so the page is at its starting state
- Demo rig should read: Time `23:00`, Battery `34%`, Progress `0%`, Drain `Steady`,
  Connectivity `Offline`
- Close other tabs; the request counter should read `0`

## Shot 1 — the decision · 0:00–0:20

**On screen:** the app at rest, Hauz Khas → Green Park.

> "Hauz Khas Village to Green Park Metro, quarter past eleven at night. Through Deer
> Park it's seventeen minutes. Along Aurobindo Marg it's twenty-three. Every navigation
> app shows you the seventeen."

*Point the cursor at the two walking cards as you say the times.*

> "Aura's first answer is actually neither of those — I'll come back to that."

*That hook pays off in shot 3. Do not explain the bus yet.*

---

## Shot 2 — Round 1, the sentence · 0:20–1:00

**Do:** click the chip **"I'm walking home alone, it's late and my phone is dying"**

> "Aura lets her say what kind of journey this is."

*Pause a beat so the chips change to the amber "read:" tags.*

> "It read four things — travelling alone, late at night, on foot, battery low — and it
> re-solved the street network against them. It now recommends the twenty-three minute
> road, and here is the reason it gives."

**Do:** point at the Why panel, then at the three factor bars.

> "Six extra minutes buys fifty-four more street lamps, sixteen more places open, and
> eight hundred and ninety fewer metres with no lighting at all. Three separate
> factors, never one safety score — because a single number hides its own assumptions.
> And there is no red and no green anywhere in this interface. Those colours are a
> verdict, and Aura doesn't issue verdicts. It counts things you could walk out and
> photograph. No crime data, no scoring of neighbourhoods."

**Do:** now click the chip **"In a hurry, I'll walk it"**

> "Change the sentence and it changes its mind. Same two walking routes, different
> priorities — now it recommends the park, and tells her plainly what she's trading:
> eight hundred and ninety metres with no lighting. It won't hide that from her, and it
> won't refuse to give her the route either. It's her journey."

## Shot 3 — the verified fleet · 1:00–1:30

**Do:** clear the input and press **Plan** to bring the bus back. Click route **A**.

> "Delhi publishes a live feed for DTC and cluster buses, so Aura can answer a question
> no other app answers: is this vehicle actually municipal?"

*Point at the two lines under the badge — the green one and the amber one.*

> "And it asks that in two parts. Is it in the city's feed — this one carries fleet
> number DL1PC-5512. And is it still *on* its published route. Because in the Nirbhaya
> case the bus was private and off its route, and that is a fact about the vehicle, not
> about who is inside it. A bus more than two hundred and fifty metres off its path gets
> refused even if it is genuinely municipal. There's an unmarked van at the same stop,
> two minutes sooner — Aura won't recommend it, and it says why."

> "We are not going to tell you how many women are on board. That needs cameras, and an
> app that announces a bus has one woman on it has built a targeting tool. What we can
> do is route you toward supervision that already exists — the Metro's women-only coach,
> a staffed gate, a marshal on the 615. And the bus is free for women, so the vehicle
> she can verify is also the fastest and the cheapest. Twelve minutes, three on foot."

## Shot 4 — nobody can poison a street · 1:30–1:55

**Do:** click the chip **"In a hurry, I'll walk it"** so the park route is selected,
then drag **Journey progress** to about `40%` so she is inside Deer Park.
Click **Add a report → 💡 Well-lit**

> "Anyone can report what they see. But this one claims a park with zero mapped lamps
> is well lit, and nobody else has said so — so Aura holds it instead of publishing it.
> One account cannot rewrite a street. You also can't pan the map somewhere else and tag
> it — you can only report the sixty metres you're standing in."

**Do:** close the modal. A purple check-in card should be showing. Click **No, it's dead**.

> "And mid-journey it asks one question, once. If she says the street isn't what Aura
> promised, her own route changes immediately — her word is always enough for her own
> path, and never enough for everybody else's. That's how you can believe the woman
> standing there without letting one account repaint a city."

*If the card says there is no better-lit route, read it out — that is the point:*

> "And here it admits there isn't a better-lit way out of the park. It won't invent one
> to look useful. It gives her the nearest lit street and the nearest open help point
> instead."

## Shot 5 — Round 2 · 1:55–2:35

**Do:** set **Battery drain → Heavy**. Wait for the amber banner to appear.

> "Now the second problem. Aura isn't watching for a percentage — it's measuring how
> fast the battery is actually falling and projecting it to her arrival."

*Point at the monospace line showing the arithmetic.*

> "At this rate she arrives on zero. She needs eight percent to still make a call. Note
> that we never assume a drain rate — every phone is different and every battery ages, so
> we measure hers."

**Do:** click **Switch to Shadow Mode**.

> "So it gives up almost everything. The map rendering is gone — the GPU was the cost,
> not the pixels. Screen black and dimmed, GPS down to once every forty-five seconds.
> What's left is one arrow, the metres remaining, the next street, and the nearest help
> point that is actually open — hand-verified, because an API that sends her to a locked
> gate at one in the morning is the worst thing this app could do."

**Do:** click **Pocket mode**, pause for two seconds, then tap to wake.

> "And the cheapest screen is no screen at all. Pocket mode puts the phone away and
> buzzes at junctions — once for left, twice for right — so she isn't lighting up a
> display and staring down at it in the dark."

**Do:** drag the Ghost Ping slider to the right.

> "And one compact check-in. Time, battery, coordinates. It opens her messaging app with
> the text already written and she presses send. It goes over SMS, so it needs no
> internet — and Aura has no ability to send anything by itself. Nothing is ever
> tracked."

---

## Shot 6 — the offline proof · 2:15–2:30

**Do:** point at the request counter reading `0`. Then turn off wi-fi and reload.

> "Zero network requests, the whole way through. Turn the internet off completely and
> reload — the map, the routing, the reasoning all still work, because they were never
> on a server."

*Let it finish loading on camera. Do not cut away.*

> "Not just where to go — which journey fits the moment. And when the signal
> disappears, the support doesn't."
